Event Overview

Join us for Stop Vibing Your Prompts — What Two Years of AI Lab Notes Taught Me, a practical look at what happens when you keep engineering-grade notes while working with large language models.

Our presenter isn’t an AI researcher and doesn’t work at a lab. They are, however, someone who has spent the past two years maintaining version histories, failure logs, and changelogs for their work with LLMs. They’ll open those notes and show what’s actually in them, with documentation behind every claim.

The through-line is simple: treat your AI setup like an incident-managed system, where every change traces back to a documented failure. The case begins with a real market-research deliverable containing a number that was wrong by about 2x—and still survived every quality check in place.

What We’ll Cover

  • The failure and the changelog entry it produced
  • The vibes loop versus the evidence loop—how many of us actually iterate on prompts, and the cheaper, better alternative
  • A quick tour of the stack that two years of this practice builds, including the one layer our presenter would defend ferociously
  • Writing rules that fire from the inside: recognition patterns versus boundary conditions
  • A working theory for why any of this works, clearly presented as a hypothesis and open to scrutiny

What You’ll Take Away

You’ll leave with three habits you can start using that night. You won’t need to copy someone else’s setup; your own documented failures can help you build a better one.

Bring your AI war stories, your skepticism—especially about the theory—and your ideas for testing the experiment that hasn’t been run yet. There will be room for questions, discussion, and thoughtful pushback.

Who Should Attend?

All skill levels are welcome. Whether you’re just beginning to experiment with AI tools or you’ve already built an elaborate LLM workflow, this meetup offers a practical framework for learning from failures and improving your process with evidence.
